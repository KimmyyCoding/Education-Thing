# Accrual Accounting

Under accrual accounting, transactions are recorded when they happen economically, not when cash moves. Revenue is recorded when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred.

This is why a company can report a profit while its bank balance falls: a sale on credit increases revenue and accounts receivable today, but the cash arrives later.

The alternative is cash-basis accounting, which only records transactions when cash is received or paid. It is simpler but gives a distorted picture of performance in any single period.

Accruals create balance sheet items such as accounts receivable, accounts payable, accrued expenses and deferred revenue.
